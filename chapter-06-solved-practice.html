A. First Law & Sign Conventions

§6.3

The first law of thermodynamics: the change in a system’s internal energy is the heat plus the work.

ΔE = q + w   |   q > 0 heat into system  ·  w > 0 work done on system

B. Pressure–Volume Work

§6.3

When a gas expands or is compressed against a constant external pressure, the work is −PΔV. Convert L·atm to joules with 1 L·atm = 101.325 J.

w = −PΔV    ΔV = Vfinal − Vinitial

C. Enthalpy & Stoichiometry

§6.4

ΔH is the heat at constant pressure, and it is extensive — it scales with the amount reacting. Convert mass → moles, then use the reaction’s ΔH as a conversion factor.

D. Calorimetry

§6.5

Heat and temperature change are linked by q = mcΔT. In a calorimeter, the heat released by the reaction is absorbed by the surroundings: qrxn = −qsurroundings.

q = m c ΔT   |   coffee-cup: qrxn = −qsoln  ·  bomb: qrxn = −CcalΔT

E. Hess’s Law

§6.6

Enthalpy is a state function, so ΔH is the same whether a reaction happens in one step or many. Work backward from the target: reverse reactions (flip the sign) and multiply (scale ΔH) until they add up to the target.

F. Standard Enthalpies of Formation

§6.6

The enthalpy of reaction equals the formation enthalpies of the products minus those of the reactants (each × its coefficient). Elements in their standard states have ΔH°f = 0.

ΔH°rxn = Σ n ΔH°f(products) − Σ m ΔH°f(reactants)
